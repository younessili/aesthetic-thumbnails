# Visual Aesthetic

Visual aesthetic influences our preferences, judgments, and overall satisfaction with our surroundings. People are naturally drawn to aesthetic. That's why aesthetic visuals make people more interested in watching a video or clicking on an image.

Visual aesthetic is based on 4 general factors.

## Factor 1: Depth

You want to immerse the viewer into a three-dimensional reality while they look at a two-dimensional screen. Visual depth can be created by making physical depth visible with the help of brightness contrast, color contrast, and sharpness contrast.

## Factor 2: Balance

Visual balance automatically creates harmony and beauty. The overall balance of an image is a result of balance within the composition, brightness, and colors.

## Factor 3: Simplicity

Complex visuals require a lot of brain power to process everything the viewer sees. People naturally value simplicity as it allows them to focus on the important subjects. Simplicity can be created by minimizing distractions within every visual element.

## Factor 4: Novelty

People don't like to see visuals they are used to, but they like to be surprised by novel visuals that they have never seen before. Novelty can be created by alternating from the norms within every visual element.

---

## Element 1: Location

### Tip 1: Assess The Shooting Potential

Maximize your creative freedom by scouting a location while thinking about the visual elements of lighting, colors, focal lengths, shot sizes, angles, compositions and movements.

### Tip 2: Check The Natural Light

As natural lighting is uncontrollable, check whether the natural light at your location allows you to achieve the look you want.

### Tip 3: Look For Physical Depth

Look for a location that has many different layers of physical depth, so that you can easily create visual depth in your image.

### Tip 4: Avoid Complexity

Look for a location that is free of distractions and random objects, so that you can easily focus the viewer's attention on your subject.

---

## Element 2: Lighting

### Tip 1: Create Brightness Contrast

Always try to create alternating patterns of dark and bright parts throughout your image to emphasize visual depth.

### Tip 2: Shoot From The Shadow Side

Adjust your camera angle to point towards the shadow side of the subject to create more depth in your image.

### Tip 3: Create Negative Fill

Use flags or other dark surfaces to absorb unwanted light reflections and further darken the shadow side of your subject.

### Tip 4: Avoid Overhead Lighting

Avoid harsh overhead lighting as it creates unappealing shadows on the face of most subjects.

### Tip 5: Think About Background Light

Create brightness contrast in the background of your image by either adding practical light sources or using external light sources.

### Tip 6: Use A Hazer

Use a hazer machine to create a soft look that simplifies the image, isolates the subject, and emphasizes lighting effects.

### Tip 7: Balance The Light Sources

Make sure to balance the brightness and color temperature of your different light sources to each other.

### Tip 8: Motivate Existing Light

Help the viewer understand where your light is coming from by adjusting visible and external light sources to each other.

---

## Element 3: Color

### Tip 1: Create Color Contrast

Create color contrast with two dominant colors to establish depth and balance throughout the image.

### Tip 2: Use Complementary Colors

It looks especially aesthetic to use pairs of complementary colors as they stand in the strongest contrast to each other.

### Tip 3: Retain Natural Skin Tones

Adjust the lighting and color grading to retain natural skin tones of orange, beige or blue while making them fit to the overall environment.

### Tip 4: Isolate Colors

To create color contrast, you can isolate single colors to achieve a monochromatic color palette and simplify your image.

### Tip 5: Maintain A Consistent Color Palette

Stay consistent with your colors when capturing multiple shots in one environment.

---

## Element 4: Actor

### Tip 1: Match The Character

Match the actor's appearance to the character and scene of your story.

### Tip 2: Scout For Novelty

Scout for unique body types, hairstyles, facial features, or tattoos as it sparks visual interest.

---

## Element 5: Outfit

### Tip 1: Create Contrast

Choose an outfit that helps separate your subject from the environment by either creating color contrast or brightness contrast.

### Tip 2: Choose Distinct Looks

Choose distinct looks that stand out from the average outfit to spark visual interest.

---

## Element 6: Action

### Tip 1: Think About The Image

The actor needs to execute actions with the image in mind so that they complement the camera's angle, composition, and movement.

### Tip 2: Give Directions

Either the director or the camera operator needs to give the actor direction on what actions to execute.

---

## Element 7: Camera Type

### Tip 1: Use Main Cameras & Drones

Main cameras and stabilized drones provide the best image quality and the most creative freedom.

### Tip 2: Use Unique Lenses

Unique lens families such as anamorphic lenses or vintage lenses allow you to create interesting visual characteristics that add novelty to your footage.

### Tip 3: Use A Diffusion Filter

A diffusion filter can help create a softer look in your image, which is often considered more aesthetic.

---

## Element 8: Settings

### Tip 1: Use A Flat Picture Profile

A flat picture profile often results in a higher dynamic range, which allows you to retain more details in the bright and dark parts of your image.

### Tip 2: Adjust The White Balance

Adjust your white balance to the environment and light situation you're shooting in.

### Tip 3: Pick The Right Frame Rate

Pick a frame rate that matches the action of your character and the moment of your story.

### Tip 4: Use The Correct Shutter Speed

Follow the 180-degree rule by setting your shutter speed to double your frame rate to achieve a natural motion blur.

### Tip 5: Use A Wide Aperture

Use a low F-Stop and wide aperture to separate the subject from the environment and create visual depth.

### Tip 6: Shoot At Native ISOs

Keep your ISO as close as possible to the native ISO of your camera and picture profile to minimize image noise.

### Tip 7: Nail The Exposure

Adjust your shutter speed, aperture, and ISO to properly expose the image.

---

## Element 9: Focal Length

### Tip 1: Use Different Focal Lengths

Capture shots on low, medium, and high focal lengths to make the visual experience more interesting and engaging.

### Tip 2: Avoid Subject Distortion

When filming a subject up close, try to avoid distortion on low focal lengths by placing the subject far from the edges, or use higher focal lengths above 35mm.

---

## Element 10: Shot Size

You can capture aesthetic shots in all shot sizes.

---

## Element 11: Angle

### Tip 1: Adjust To The Environment

Adjust your angle to the environment, so that the background of your image includes the most physical depth, brightness contrast, and color contrast.

### Tip 2: Match The Height

A straight angle will often provide the most physical depth in your background. Match the height of your camera to the subject.

### Tip 3: Use Unconventional Angles

Experiment with unconventional angles to create novelty and curiosity in the viewer.

---

## Element 12: Composition

### Rule 1: Pick A Dominant Subject

Pick a dominant subject to establish a visual hierarchy and steer the viewer's attention to one part of the frame.

### Rule 2: Follow The Rule Of Thirds

Place the elements within your composition on the lines or intersecting points of the grid to create visual balance.

### Rule 3: Consider Leading And Headroom

Leave enough leading room in front of your subject, and find a good balance for the headroom above your subject.

### Rule 4: Look For Leading Lines

Look for compositions where the lines in your environment guide the viewer's attention towards the subject.

### Rule 5: Use Symmetry

Scan your environment and subject for horizontal or vertical symmetries as perfectly balanced compositions look naturally aesthetic.

### Rule 6: Fill The Frame

Fill the frame with important information and avoid empty or unimportant spaces that throw off the balance.

### Rule 7: Avoid Distractions

Keep unimportant distractions out of the background as they pull the viewer's attention away from the intended part of your composition.

### Rule 8: Create Visual Depth

Actively include different layers of the environment into your composition, so the viewer can see the depth between your foreground, middle ground, and background.

### Rule 9: Frame The Subject

To draw even more attention to the subject, frame your subject with natural frames of the environment.

### Rule 10: Focus On Repetition

Repeating patterns generally look visually appealing and create an extreme amount of balance and order.

---

## Element 13: Movement

### Tip 1: Avoid Micro Jitters

Use in-body-stabilization, post-stabilization, and specific shooting techniques to minimize distracting vibrations.

### Tip 2: Match The Direction

Match the direction and speed of your movement to the action of your subject as it keeps the subject centered while sparking visual interest in the background.

### Tip 3: Use Opposite Rotations

Rotate your camera opposite to the subject's rotation as it reveals different sides of the subject and makes movements look more dynamic.

---

## Element 14: Focus

### Tip 1: Keep It Sharp

Keep the most important area of your image sharp at all times.

### Tip 2: Avoid Focus Breathing

Avoid your auto-focus from shifting between different subjects by using the right auto-focus mode.

### Tip 3: Shift The Focus Smoothly

If you execute focus shifts from one subject to another, make sure to use the right focus speed and nail the focus at the start and end.
