# Visual Psychology

Visual psychology is about the relationship between visual language and human perception.

Through past experiences, we make emotional connections to visuals and sounds. As a video or image is a visual experience, it is basically an alternate reality in which you immerse the viewer. You need to reverse-engineer those connections and show the right visuals to trigger the right emotions.

Visual language is a summary of elements that influence the environment, subjects, and experience of reality. These elements include the location, lighting and colors, body and head movements, ability to focus attention, and many more.

Each visual element is a tool to psychologically influence the reality of the viewer. The more psychological connections you understand, the better you can communicate your story.

---

## Element 1: Location

Importance: High

### Emotion

The location evokes specific emotions in the viewer.

Natural Locations:

Beach → Relaxation, Joy

Top Of Mountain → Triumph, Freedom

Cave → Depression, Claustrophobia

Human-Made Locations:

New And Modern Apartment → Freshness, Sterility

Ordinary Living Room With Plants → Comfort, Groundedness

Royal Suite With Chandeliers → Luxury, Elegance, Superiority

The overall feeling of a location is described as the "Mood". The mood is a summary of the emotional connections we make to objects, shapes, materials, and colors.

Objects:

Fire Place → Warmth, Comfort

Dolls And Masks → Fear, Paranoia

Shapes:

Organic → Harmony, Dynamic

Geometrical → Stability, Order

Materials:

Cement → Discomfort, Coldness

Wood → Comfort, Warmth

Colors:

Black → Power, Authority, Mystery

Gold → Wealth, Luxury, Prestige

### Character Building

The location helps with character building as the home of a character is an embodiment of their identity and values.

Tidy And Organized → Punctuality, Order, Professionalism

Messy And Disorganized → Chaotic, Laziness, Overwhelm

## Element 2: Lighting

Importance: High

### Natural Lighting

The different stages of natural lighting influence the emotions of a scene.

Nighttime → Dark, Isolated, Depressing, Eerie, Scary, Quiet, Mysterious

Blue Hour → Awakened, Fresh, Ambitious / Cold, Sentimental, Reflective, Calm

Golden Hour → Warm, Energetic, Happy, Epic, Alive, Beautiful, Strong

Sunny Daytime → Uplifting, Bright, Cheering, Comforting, Ordinary, Conservative

Cloudy Daytime → Dull, Plain, Colorless, Depressing, Muted, Boring

### Factor 1: Position

The light position influences the conveyed emotions and intentions of a character.

Front Light → Openness, Clarity, Approachability, Trust

Side Light → Dramatic, Mysterious, Secretive

Back Light → Ethereal, Dreamlike, Mysterious

Top Light → Intense, Authoritative, Commanding

Bottom Light → Haunting, Eerie, Fearful

Catch Light → Energetic, Alive, Hopeful

No Catch Light → Sad, Dead, Evil

### Factor 2: Brightness

The overall brightness of light influences the energy of a scene.

Complete Darkness → Eerie, Suspenseful, Mysterious, Fearful, Claustrophobic

Low Brightness → Cozy, Intimate, Calm, Relaxing, Reflective, Melancholic

Medium Brightness → Natural, Ordinary, Conservative

High Brightness → Joyful, Uplifting, Energetic, Optimistic, Lively

Extreme Brightness → Overwhelming, Blinding, Astonishing

The individual brightness of subjects can be used for visual storytelling.

Darkness → Uncertainty → Tension

Brightness → Certainty → Resolution

### Factor 3: Hardness

Soft Light → Comforting, Calm, Gentle, Romantic, Dreamy

Normal Light → Balanced, Neutral, Authentic, Relatable

Hard Light → Intense, Dramatic, Edgy, Aggressive

### Factor 4: Focus

Narrow Focus → Isolated Attention / Dramatic, Intense, Exaggerated, Intriguing

Normal Focus → Balanced, Natural, Authentic, Realistic, Familiar

Wide Focus → Evenly Distributed Attention / Expansive, Open, Grand, Vast

### Factor 5: Color

Orange Colors → Warm → Cozy, Inviting, Comforting, Intimate, Romantic

Neutral Colors → Balanced, Natural, Calm, Serene, Subtle

Blue Colors → Cold → Mysterious, Moody, Sad, Melancholic / Refreshing, Reflective

## Element 5: Outfit

Importance: Medium

### Character Building

The outfit of a character communicates their identity, personality, occupation, social status, and social background.

Casual Red Shirt → Soft, Emotional, Loving

Muted Hoodie → Undercover, Isolated, Introverted

Plain Shirt And Tie → Conventional, Mundane, Monotonous

Hawaii Shirt And Leather Jacket → Unconventional, Rebellious, Anarchic

### Character Transformation

The change of outfit can visually support character transformation.

Worn Out Blanket → Low Status

Leather Jacket → Medium Status

Tailored Suit → High Status

## Element 6: Action

Importance: High

The action of a character communicates their emotions.

Laughing → Joy, Happiness, Amusement

Crying → Sadness, Grief, Despair, Vulnerability

Screaming → Fear, Terror, Shock

Whispering → Intimacy, Secrecy, Intrigue, Suspense

Pulling Up Sleeves → Motivation, Readiness, Determination

Leaning Head On Hands → Contemplation, Stress, Anxiety

Tapping Fingers → Nervousness, Anticipation

Putting Hands Behind Head → Relaxation, Relief

The nuances of an action also impact the conveyed emotions.

Walking Slowly → Unhurried, Calm, Relaxed

Walking Quickly → Determined, Rushed, Stressed

Waiting Patiently → Calm, Composed, Collected

Waiting Impatiently → Restless, Hurried, Nervous

## Element 8: Settings

Importance: Medium

### White Balance

Cold Color Temperature → Mysterious, Moody, Sad, Melancholic / Refreshing, Reflective

Neutral Color Temperature → Balanced, Natural, Calm, Serene, Subtle

Warm Color Temperature → Cozy, Inviting, Comforting, Intimate, Romantic

### Frame Rate

Real-Time (24/25 FPS) → Realistic Speed Of Time → No Emphasis

Slow Motion (> 24/25 FPS) → Stretched Speed Of Time → Emphasis On The Moment

Time Lapse (< 24/25 FPS) → Compressed Speed Of Time → Emphasis On Transformation

### Shutter Speed

Slow Shutter Speed → Unfocused, Relaxed, Drugged

Normal Shutter Speed → Natural

Fast Shutter Speed → Focused, Adrenaline-Loaded

### Aperture

Small Aperture / High F-Stop → More Attention On The Environment

Wide Aperture / Low F-Stop → Less Attention On The Environment

High Angle → Vulnerable, Submissive, Weak, Oppressed, Powerless, Small

Straight Angles → Balance Of Power

## Element 12: Composition

Importance: High

### Relationships

One Character → Isolation, Loneliness

Two Characters Close Together → Connection, Trust

Two Characters Far Apart → Disconnection, Lack Of Trust

### Emotions

Balanced Compositions → Harmony, Focus, Aesthetic, Positivity

Unbalanced Compositions → Disruption, Confusion, Ugliness, Negativity

### Rules Of Balance

1. Pick A Dominant Subject
2. Follow The Rule Of Thirds
3. Consider Leading And Head Room
4. Look For Leading Lines
5. Use Symmetry
6. Fill The Frame
7. Avoid Distractions
8. Create Visual Depth
9. Frame The Subject
10. Focus On Repetition

### Rules Of Imbalance

Each rule of balance can be purposely broken to create imbalance.

1. Pick No Dominant Subject
2. Ignore The Rule Of Thirds
3. Disrupt Leading And Head Room
4. Look For Misleading Lines
5. Use Asymmetry
6. Leave Empty Space
7. Embrace Distractions
8. Flatten The Image
9. Isolate The Subject
10. Break Repetition

## Element 13: Movement

Importance: High

### Factor 1: Style

Tripod → Stable, Calm, Controlled, Objective, Professional

Handheld → Natural, Organic, Authentic, Intimate, Raw

Stabilized → Polished, Balanced, Graceful, Elegant, Precise

### Factor 2: Speed

Slow Speed → Low Intensity / Calm, Relaxing, Contemplative, Tense

Medium Speed → Medium Intensity / Balanced, Harmonious, Engaging

Fast Speed → High Intensity / Energetic, Exciting, Thrilling, Dynamic

### Factor 3: Direction

Forward → Progressive / Welcoming, Engaging, Advancing, Focused

Backward → Regressive / Reflective, Reversing, Nostalgic

Sideward Right → Transitory, Progressive

Sideward Left → Transitory, Regressive

Upward → Empowering, Ascending, Optimistic, Freeing

Downward → Surrendering, Descending, Overwhelming, Restraining

### Factor 4: Rotation

Pan Right → Progressive, Advancing

Pan Left → Regressive, Reversing

Tilt Up → Increasing Dominance

Tilt Down → Decreasing Dominance

Roll Right → Progressive / Disorienting

Roll Left → Regressive / Disorienting

### Factor 5: Zoom

Zoom In → Increasing Attention Towards Middle Of The Frame / Tension

Zoom Out → Decreasing Attention From the Middle Of The Frame / Resolution

---

## Element 14: Focus

Importance: Low

In Focus → Attention / Certainty

Out Of Focus → No Attention / Uncertainty
