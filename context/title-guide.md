# YouTube Title Guide

Titles are packaging — not SEO. A title's job is to complete the promise that the thumbnail starts, while accurately representing the video the viewer is about to watch.

---

## The Title-Thumbnail-Video Triangle

Every click decision has three parts. All three must deliver the same promise:

1. **Thumbnail** — stops the scroll. Creates an emotional reaction or curiosity. Shows, doesn't tell.
2. **Title** — explains the promise. Tells the viewer what they'll get. Adds information the thumbnail can't convey.
3. **Video** — delivers on the promise. If the video doesn't match what the thumbnail + title promised, you lose trust and get punished by the algorithm (low retention, low satisfaction).

**The rule:** If you can't draw a straight line from thumbnail → title → video content, something is broken. A killer thumbnail-title combo that oversells the video is worse than a mediocre one that's honest.

### How thumbnail and title work together

They are NOT separate creative decisions. They're two halves of one message:

- **Thumbnail shows, title tells.** The thumbnail creates an emotional/visual hook. The title adds the specific information. Together they form a complete pitch.
- **They must not repeat each other.** If the thumbnail has text saying "AI REPLACED ME" and the title says "How AI Is Replacing Operations Managers" — that's redundant. The thumbnail should show the feeling (a person dissolving into data), and the title should state the specific claim.
- **The title fills the gap the thumbnail leaves.** Look at the thumbnail — what question does it raise? The title answers just enough to get the click, not so much that there's nothing left to watch.

**Example:**
- Thumbnail: A creator standing in a vast empty office, every desk cleared out (feeling: something dramatic happened here)
- Title: "How AI Is Replacing the $100K Operations Manager" (information: oh, AI is why the office is empty — and it's about a specific, expensive role)
- Together: emotional hook + specific promise = click

**Anti-example:**
- Thumbnail: A person with a shocked face pointing at text "AI TOOK MY JOB"
- Title: "AI Is Taking Operations Jobs — Here's What To Do"
- Together: redundant — both say the same thing. No curiosity gap. Feels like clickbait.

## Generating Titles From a Thumbnail Concept

When you have a generated thumbnail image, create titles using this process:

### 1. Read the thumbnail
What does this image SHOW? What emotion does it create? What question does it raise in the viewer's mind?

### 2. Read the video outline
What is this video actually about? What's the core takeaway? What will the viewer learn or feel by the end?

### 3. Find the gap
What information does the viewer need (that the thumbnail doesn't provide) to decide "I need to watch this"? The title fills that gap.

### 4. Write 3 options
Each title should:
- Complete the thumbnail's message (not repeat it)
- Accurately represent the video content
- Front-load the hook (first 5-6 words must work alone — mobile truncation)
- Be specific over vague ("$100K Operations Manager" > "Your Operations Role")

### 5. Check the triangle
For each title, ask: Does this thumbnail + this title accurately represent what happens in this video? If a viewer clicks and watches — will they feel the promise was kept?

## What Makes Titles Work

### Specificity
Specific beats vague every time. Numbers, dollar amounts, timeframes, and named things draw the eye and build credibility.

- "The $100K Operations Manager" > "The Operations Manager"
- "3 Systems Every Creator Needs Before Scaling" > "Systems Every Creator Needs"
- "Why Creator Businesses Break at $500K" > "Why Creator Businesses Fail"

### Promise clarity
The viewer should know what they'll get. Not cleverness for its own sake — clarity of value.

- Clear: "How to Fire Yourself From Your Own Business" (I'll learn how to step back)
- Unclear: "The Great Unbundling" (of what? why should I care?)

### Curiosity gap (not clickbait)
The best titles make you NEED to know the answer — but only when the video actually delivers that answer.

- Good gap: "Why I Left a 7-Figure Business to Start My Own" (makes you wonder: why would someone leave success?)
- Clickbait: "You Won't BELIEVE What Happened to My Business" (no specific promise, just hype)

### Front-loading
Mobile truncation cuts titles around 50-60 characters. The first 5-6 words must carry the hook.

- Front-loaded: "How AI Is Replacing the $100K Operations Manager"
- Buried hook: "Here's What I Think About How AI Is Changing Operations Management"

## Title Patterns

Starting points to riff on — not rules to follow. Use these as a launching pad, then check against the triangle.

| Pattern | Example | Why it works |
|---------|---------|-------------|
| **Bold claim + context** | "How AI Is Replacing the $100K Operations Manager" | Specific, provocative, searchable |
| **Number + outcome** | "5 Automations Every Creator Business Needs" | Scannable, promise is clear |
| **"I [did thing]"** | "I Built an AI Agent That Runs a Creator's Newsletter" | Personal proof, curiosity gap |
| **Question** | "Do You Even Need an Operator?" | Speaks directly to viewer's internal debate |
| **Problem + fix** | "Why Most Creator Businesses Break at $500K" | Pain-forward, viewer self-selects |
| **Transformation** | "How to Fire Yourself From Your Own Business" | Clear before/after, aspirational |

### What to stop doing

| Old pattern | Problem | Fix |
|-------------|---------|-----|
| Long subtitles after dashes | Dash splits attention, second half gets truncated on mobile | One clean title, no dashes. Use parentheses for context if needed |
| "How I [vague thing]" | No specific outcome stated | Be specific about the result: what changed? |
| "Template Included" / bonus bait | Bonus isn't a hook — it's a bonus | Lead with the value, mention bonus in description |

## Technical Rules

- **Under 60 characters** when possible — longer titles get truncated on mobile
- **No colons in regular video titles** — save "TOPIC: Subtitle" for courses only
- **Parenthetical context at the end** — "(2026)", "(for Creators)" adds info without cluttering the hook
- **Year tags on evergreen content** — "(2026)" signals freshness and helps search
- **Dollar amounts and specific numbers** when relevant — they draw the eye

---

## What to Avoid

- **Repeating the thumbnail text in the title.** They're a package, not two versions of the same thing.
- **Vague superlatives.** "Amazing", "incredible", "game-changing" — says nothing.
- **Dashes that split the title.** The part after the dash often gets truncated and splits attention.
- **Overselling.** If the title promises more than the video delivers, retention drops and the algorithm punishes you. Slightly underselling with a great video > overselling with a decent one.
- **Generic "How I" openings** without a specific outcome. "How I Built Self-awareness" — what changed? Be specific about the result.
